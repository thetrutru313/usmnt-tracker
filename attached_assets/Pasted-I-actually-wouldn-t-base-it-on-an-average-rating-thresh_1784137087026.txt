I actually wouldn't base it on an average rating threshold alone.

The problem is that a **7.2 average means something different** for:

* a striker (who can oscillate between 6.4 and 8.8),
* a center back (who often lives between 6.8 and 7.4),
* a player in the Premier League versus the Eredivisie,
* someone coming off the bench versus playing 90 minutes.

Instead, I'd treat "Hot/Steady/Cold" as **form relative to the player's own baseline**, much like clubs do.

## Recommended model

Calculate three things:

1. **5-match rolling average** (current form)
2. **Season average** (baseline)
3. **Trend** (last 5 vs previous 5)

Then score the player based on all three.

### Step 1: Rolling average

```
Current Form = Average of last 5 ratings
```

Five matches is long enough to smooth randomness but short enough to capture momentum.

---

### Step 2: Season baseline

```
Season Average = Average of all ratings this season
```

---

### Step 3: Trend

```
Trend = Last 5 Avg − Previous 5 Avg
```

This captures whether they're improving or declining.

Example:

| Last 5 | Previous 5 | Trend |
| ------ | ---------- | ----- |
| 7.52   | 6.94       | +0.58 |
| 6.84   | 7.31       | -0.47 |

This is much more meaningful than "7.3 = hot."

---

# Suggested labels

## 🔥 Rising / Hot

All of these:

* Last 5 Avg ≥ 7.30
* Trend ≥ +0.30
* Played at least 4 of last 5 matches

OR

* Last 5 Avg is **0.40 above season average**

Example

Season avg: 6.86

Last five:

7.3
7.5
7.8
7.1
7.4

Last 5 avg = 7.42

Difference = +0.56

→ 🔥 Hot

---

## ➖ Steady

Current form within ±0.30 of season average

Example

Season avg = 7.02

Last 5 = 6.94

Difference = -0.08

→ Steady

---

## 🥶 Falling / Cold

Either

* Last 5 Avg ≤ 6.70

OR

* Last 5 Avg is 0.40 below season average

Example

Season avg = 7.15

Last 5 = 6.61

Difference = -0.54

→ Cold

---

# Better: Use percentiles

Since ratings vary by provider and position, normalize every player against themselves.

Example:

Difference between Last 5 and Season

| Difference     | Label       |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| +0.50+         | 🔥 Very Hot |
| +0.30 to +0.49 | 🔥 Hot      |
| -0.29 to +0.29 | ➖ Steady    |
| -0.30 to -0.49 | 🥶 Cold     |
| -0.50+         | ❄️ Ice Cold |

This works whether a player averages 6.6 or 7.4.

---

# Add "confidence"

A player who has played only 2 matches shouldn't be labeled "Hot."

Example:

| Minutes in last 5 | Confidence      |
| ----------------- | --------------- |
| 400–450           | ★★★★★           |
| 300–399           | ★★★★☆           |
| 180–299           | ★★★☆☆           |
| 90–179            | ★★☆☆☆           |
| <90               | Not enough data |

Or require at least 270 minutes over the last five matches before assigning a form label.

---

# Bonus: Form score

Instead of fixed labels, compute a score from -100 to +100.

```
Form Score =
50 × (Last5Avg − SeasonAvg)
+
30 × (Last5Avg − Previous5Avg)
+
20 × WinContribution
```

Then classify:

| Score       | Status      |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| 60 to 100   | 🔥 On Fire  |
| 25 to 59    | 📈 Rising   |
| -24 to 24   | ➖ Steady    |
| -59 to -25  | 📉 Falling  |
| -100 to -60 | 🥶 Ice Cold |

This gives you a continuous metric that's easy to sort and visualize.

## If I were building a USMNT player tracker

I'd show four metrics on each player card:

* **Form:** Last 5 average rating (e.g., 7.46)
* **Trend:** Change from previous 5 matches (e.g., +0.42)
* **Season:** Season average rating (e.g., 6.98)
* **Status:** 🔥 Rising, ➖ Steady, or 🥶 Falling

That approach avoids penalizing naturally lower-rated positions, captures genuine momentum, and provides a clear, intuitive snapshot of each player's club form as you track the player pool toward the 2030 World Cup.
